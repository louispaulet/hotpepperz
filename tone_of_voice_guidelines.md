# HotPepperz Tone of Voice Guidelines

## Summary
HotPepperz should read like a premium editorial reference about chili culture: vivid, informed, and sensory, but never gimmicky. The site can sound warm and stylish, yet the writing should always help the reader understand peppers, pairings, kitchens, and regional context more clearly.

The strongest current copy already points in this direction when it is precise, culinary, and image-rich without becoming showy. The weakest copy drifts into product-architecture explanations, generic UX language, or stunt-heat jokes that make the brand feel less credible.

## Brand essence
- Editorial reference, not novelty brand.
- Pepper-first and food-literate.
- Confident and cultivated, without sounding academic for its own sake.
- Sensory and specific, without inflating every sentence.

## Core tone attributes
- Vivid: language should help the reader picture flavor, climate, texture, or use.
- Precise: prefer culinary specificity over generic “experience” or “destination” wording.
- Expert: sound like an informed editor or careful cook, not a hype marketer.
- Clear: navigation and interface copy should stay easy to scan.
- Disciplined: metaphors can add character, but they should not overpower the meaning.

## What To Optimize For
- Use concrete food and pepper vocabulary: aroma, flesh, finish, acid, climate, texture, roast, ferment, table sauce, broth, herb, fruit.
- Keep UI copy short and directional.
- Let editorial copy carry the richness while interface copy stays understated.
- Explain why something matters in culinary or cultural terms.
- Preserve a sense of craft in the workshop: practical, credible, and bench-ready.

## What To Avoid
- Overexplaining information architecture with phrases like “route-driven,” “destination,” “category behavior,” “hierarchy,” or “where to click next” unless absolutely needed.
- Generic premium filler such as “clearer roles,” “structured front door,” or “behaves more like.”
- Novelty-heat comedy that undermines the reference tone.
- Mixed metaphors or flourishes that say less than a simpler sentence would.
- Legal copy rewritten into brand language. Legal copy should be plain, compliant, and readable.

## Sub-Voices
### Brand and UI voice
- Short, clear, calm, and editorial.
- Prefer direct labels like “Open the atlas” or “Browse pepper profiles.”
- Avoid describing the interface mechanics unless the user truly needs orientation.

### Editorial reference voice
- Richer and more atmospheric, but still grounded in real food and place.
- Center peppers, flavor behavior, kitchen use, agricultural setting, and cultural context.
- Avoid dramatic language that treats heat as a dare or gimmick.

### Workshop voice
- Practical, craft-led, and confident.
- Sound like a careful sauce maker giving usable direction.
- Keep playful lines under control; the workshop should feel serious enough to trust.

### Legal voice
- Separate from the brand voice.
- Prioritize clarity, accuracy, and compliance framing.
- Remove awkward phrasing or placeholders that feel unfinished, but do not try to make legal copy “stylish.”

## Writing Rules
- Prefer nouns and verbs over abstract framing.
- Prefer one strong image over stacked metaphors.
- Replace platform language with culinary language when possible.
- If a line can be made shorter without losing meaning, shorten it.
- If a sentence claims clarity, structure, or hierarchy, check whether it should instead name the actual content value.

## Do / Don’t Examples
- Do: “Profiles now prioritize origin, heat, climate, pairings, and related reading so users can scan the key reference cues quickly.”
- Don’t: “Use this page as the structured front door to the encyclopedia.”

- Do: “A fruit-and-fire pairing that works because tropical sweetness broadens aroma before the heat finishes the sentence.”
- Don’t: “The first bite can be a handshake; the next thirty seconds are the lawsuit.”

- Do: “Use accents the way a careful cook seasons a pot.”
- Don’t: “A serious green sauce should not wear the costume of a prank bottle.”

- Do: “This page is a France-oriented compliance draft for an editorial website.”
- Don’t: “This text is provided as a compliance-oriented editorial draft for a France-facing site.”

## Replacement Framework
- De-productize: remove copy about routes, destination logic, or interface behavior when the user benefit can be stated more directly.
- Tighten: shorten UI and section-intro copy to the clearest useful statement.
- De-hype: replace stunt, swagger, or joke language with sharper culinary specificity.
- Clarify: rewrite vague premium phrasing into concrete meaning.
- Legal-clarify: keep compliance intent intact while making the language plainer and easier to trust.
